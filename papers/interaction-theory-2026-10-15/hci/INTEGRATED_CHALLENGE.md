# Focused HCI challenge of the integrated interaction theory

2026-10-15. One conceptual reading of `docs/INTERACTION_THEORY.md` after the
four research contributions were integrated, before the separate aviation
worked case. No product tests, further literature acquisition, or review of
future reports were performed. This challenges the theory's explanatory and
generative content; it is not an implementation qualification.

## Judgment

The document is an explicit working interaction theory rather than a renamed
feature list. It defines the work episode, constructs, proposed mechanisms,
values, predictions, limits, and counterexamples. Its explanatory content is
strongest where two designs offer the same facts or permissions yet impose
different reconstruction, activation, target ambiguity, or effect uncertainty.
Those differences can generate genuinely discriminating design choices.

The wider account preserves important aims that a compulsory feedback loop
would lose. Delegation permits attention elsewhere; understanding through
representational action is productive work; appropriation can alter the
instrument; beauty has independent experiential value. Human/model functional
coordination is repeatedly distinguished from equivalent cognition or identical
presentation. No correction is needed on these points.

Two modest additions would make the account harder to rationalize after seeing
an outcome and better preserve its ambitious generative aim.

## 1. State how a local causal claim can lose

The predictions often qualify their subjects with terms such as appropriate,
relevant, useful, or consequential. Those are necessary task-relative judgments,
but they can also absorb every failure. For example, a composition that causes
more errors could be retrospectively excluded as an inappropriate composition.
The final boundary statement acknowledges this risk without giving a concrete
rule for revising a design claim.

Suggested addition after the derivation paragraph, in substance:

> For a concrete comparison, state the episode, compared alternatives,
> expected mechanism, and important costs before judging the result. If the
> proposed change does not remove the burden it was meant to remove, or
> introduces a more consequential burden, reject or narrow that local design
> claim. Do not rescue it by redefining successful work or the participant's
> intention afterward. A counterexample can change the mechanism or its
> applicability; it need not invalidate every proposition in the theory.

This is a reasoning discipline, not a requirement for another test framework,
numeric score, or evaluation gate. It makes useful falsification possible
without pretending that an entire plural account can be rejected by one task.

## 2. Compare newly possible methods as well as efficiency on familiar work

The thesis says a good instrument can open useful possibilities. Most
discriminating questions subsequently ask whether participants perform an
already expressible task with less reconstruction or error. This is valuable,
but a design could win those comparisons by narrowing its repertoire and still
fail the operator's expressiveness and experimentation aims.

Suggested addition to the agency question or worked consequences:

> Also ask which useful intentions become expressible, and which disappear,
> when the repertoire changes. Compare the ability to invent and revise a
> method, including combinations the interface author did not preselect;
> speed on familiar tasks alone cannot decide that question.

A concrete example would be selecting a newly defined set of work objects,
applying one composed investigation, inspecting its partial results, and
revising the selection or instrument without reconstructing the episode.
The theory does not need to prescribe that exact interaction. It needs the
comparison to count increases and losses in expressive possibility as outcomes,
alongside costs incurred by learning and maintaining the added instrument.

## Scope of this challenge

Neither finding blocks writing or using the theory. Both are concrete
refinements to the derivation of design claims. The current account already
avoids claims that every frame must be monitored, every model tool resembles a
human gesture, every aesthetic choice needs productivity proof, or every
customization requires permission. Preserve those distinctions when adding a
worked example from a highly trained operational setting.
