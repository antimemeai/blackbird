# Joint work, causal feedback and progress

2026-10-15. Independent systems researcher/frumentarius contribution to an explicit Blackbird interaction theory. This is a theoretical proposal grounded in current requirements, primary literature and read-only source inspection. It chooses no TUI architecture, dependency, implementation order or numerical latency requirement. The other research lanes address human cognition, embodied controls and model interaction.

## The claim

Blackbird should support **joint work in which participants can identify what they are working on, change its course, discover what their actions actually did, and continue or repair the work while other activity proceeds**. Its interface is part of that work: a set of representations and instruments through which participants coordinate. A screen of messages alone does not supply coordination, and permitting arbitrary actions alone does not supply agency that can be exercised intelligently.

The earlier observe/target/act/understand-feedback loop is a useful local description. It misses three things when treated as the whole theory: people and models coordinate with others, an action can complete long after its initiating view disappears, and an apparently completed interaction may leave unresolved effects. Add **coordinate, continue and repair** around the local loop. These are recurring activities, rather than a mandatory seven-step interaction sequence.

The intended result is useful, expressive work with low coordination cost. Rich presentation can make relationships perceptible and controls pleasant to inhabit; its benefit is not exhausted by a throughput score. The operator's aesthetic and expressive aims are explicit values. This lane establishes constraints on truthful effects and sustained control, rather than deriving the value of beauty from distributed-systems papers.

## What the primary sources contribute

Three originals were acquired intact. The [TSV manifest](sources.tsv) records their URLs, versions, dates, byte counts, hashes and exact restoration commands. PDFs are ignored; local extracted reading copies live under ignored `quarantine/interaction-theory-2026-10-15/systems/`. These brief source accounts distinguish the published arguments from the Blackbird proposals that follow.

**Joint activity.** Klein, Woods, Bradshaw, Hoffman and Feltovich frame coordinated work through mutual predictability, directability, common ground, attention management and the cost of coordination. Their shared-display example explicitly separates putting an event on a display from knowing that a colleague noticed it. They also warn that adaptable automation can become less predictable. This is a conceptual framework informed by earlier work, rather than a quantitative study of current coding models. Its relevance here is the need for interpretable coordination signals and repair. It does not establish that an LLM has human beliefs, intentions, commitments or attention. Blackbird can expose declared goals, work state, message disposition and observed behavior without claiming access to an inner mental state. [Ten Challenges for Making Automation a Team Player in Joint Human-Agent Activity, 2004, pp. 91–95](https://www.ihmc.us/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/17.-Team-Players.pdf).

**Application completion.** Saltzer, Reed and Clark argue that some guarantees require endpoint knowledge that an intermediate subsystem lacks. Delivery acknowledgment is their particularly relevant example: a delivered message does not establish that the target application performed the requested action. Their discussion of duplicate suppression likewise separates network duplicates from newly originated application retries. They retain lower-level mechanisms when those improve performance, and explicitly make the argument conditional on identifying the application's endpoints. For Blackbird this motivates naming each claimed result and observing the component that can establish it; it does not prescribe moving every function upward, removing native guarantees, or adding acknowledgments to every routine action. [End-to-End Arguments in System Design, 1984 author-hosted reprint, especially pp. 4–7](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/endtoend/endtoend.pdf).

**Known order.** Lamport distinguishes causal partial order from an imposed total order. Local event order and message send/receive links establish known ordering; an ordering produced from logical clocks need not identify a causal influence in the reverse direction. His event-granularity footnote matters: receipt at an interrupt and handling the interrupt are different events. This gives a precise foundation for separating acquisition, handling, effect and feedback. It neither makes every UI a distributed consensus problem nor requires vector clocks or a global logical-clock service. Blackbird's existing operation IDs, original references and explicit causal links already express relevant distinctions. [Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System, 1978, especially pp. 558–561 and conclusion](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/time-clocks.pdf).

## The unit of work and its representations

The unit is an **ongoing activity undertaken for an operator purpose**, involving participants, programs, tools and artifacts. Examples include repairing a failing build, conducting a disputed code review, comparing designs with several models, or changing Blackbird's own workflow. It may cross turns, terminals, machines, contexts and executable replacements. A conversation is one coordination channel within it; a fixed turn alternation or delegation tree cannot define its limits.

For design reasoning, describe an activity using its purpose and constraints, participating actors, addressed objects, actions in progress, observed results, outstanding uncertainty, and ways to continue. These are conceptual distinctions, not a request for one universal activity database or a mandatory object schema.

The object of an action must survive the representation used to select it. A row, pane position, hotkey slot, PID or MIDI port index is a way to locate something at a moment. It is not necessarily that thing's identity. If a collaborator inserts a row or a controller reconnects, a held gesture must not silently acquire a different object. Conversely, requiring a full state-version match for every action is excessive: asking to inspect a stable job ID can remain meaningful after its output changes. A write that depends on a particular revision needs that dependency carried and checked; an identity-only operation need not pretend to depend on every displayed field.

Human, model and Lua representations may differ. They need compatible object/effect meanings and usable paths to relevant originals. A model need not receive the entire screen; a human need not read the wire representation. A summary is successful when it supports its intended judgments and offers retrieval or repair when it cannot. Neither a shared display nor inclusion in a model request proves the recipient understood it. Avoid read-status claims stronger than the available observations.

## An action has several relevant endpoints

Consider stopping an expensive command from a pedal while a model is streaming output. Distinguish the physical transition, device/terminal acquisition, gesture interpretation, resolved operation and target, acceptance or rejection, requested effect, observed process/service outcome, and participant-facing feedback. These may happen in different components and at different times.

Their causal connection, rather than screen proximity, explains the action. A stop label becoming animated proves that a representation changed. A signal syscall returning proves the corresponding local signal operation completed under its OS semantics. Neither proves remote work was undone. A cancellation result can truthfully state that local client activity settled while the remote outcome remains unknown. Pending, failed and unknown are useful states of work, not blemishes to paint over.

Feedback should answer the question relevant to the action. Fast local echo supports continuous steering; an acknowledged submission supports deciding whether to submit again; an observed settled outcome supports continuation. These do not require three banners or three ritual acknowledgments. A compact representation can reveal the distinction only when it matters. Haptic feedback also needs a declared meaning: a press pulse and a completed-stop pulse cannot silently stand for the same thing.

An interruption request may race completion. The interface should expose the resulting state, preserving completed work and the recorded request, rather than manufacture a cancellation success because the request arrived. Similarly, replacing a view, detaching a client or reloading a Lua definition must not erase an already accepted operation's obligations.

## Progress under competition

Responsiveness is a relationship between an intended action, its feedback, concurrent work and a declared operating envelope. Average frame rate and raw decoder speed cannot alone characterize it. Control acquisition, interpretation, acceptance, effect and feedback each have separate delays and failure modes.

The normative demand is **continued opportunity to exercise the controls that the interface promises while permitted work competes for resources**. A design can meet it using bounded work, preemption, cancellation, isolation, or a narrower supported workload. A separate input thread is insufficient if handling still waits on a render lock. A coroutine budget checked between resumptions cannot stop a native function that never returns. Hard guarantees require actual assumptions about scheduling, blocking calls, storage and terminal/device behavior.

A permanently stalled terminal cannot display a new acknowledgment on that terminal. The meaningful promise might preserve input acceptance, permit effect interruption and present the truthful latest state when output resumes; an independent feedback channel might offer more. The theory must not promise visible instantaneous feedback through a channel that cannot transmit. It must also not treat a hardware disconnect as if a held pedal remained physically asserted forever.

Do not equate all queued bytes with the same obligation. Original application observations and emitted raw metrics retain their existing preservation contracts. A current display may supersede unstarted obsolete presentations. A partly emitted terminal packet has a different obligation: its remaining suffix and ordering must preserve parser/screen correctness. Relative motion may be aggregatable only if the operation's semantics permit it; summing deltas preserves neither every intermediate preview nor necessarily an acceleration-dependent result. Button transitions, admission decisions and completed effects need their actual semantics preserved.

There is no general combination of unbounded production, indefinitely unavailable consumers, finite storage and lossless retention. Name where backpressure, refusal or producer disposition occurs. The operator's audit contract and raw recorder rules govern those channels; display coalescing is not permission to sample originals or metric events.

## Continuity is semantic, not an image of the old screen

An activity continues when its purpose, relevant artifacts, accepted actions, results, pending changes and unresolved effects remain usable to its participants. Reconstructing identical pixels after restart is neither necessary nor sufficient. A restored draft and selected original can matter more than restoring the last animation phase.

Definitions and mappings can change during work without changing the meaning of already dispatched actions. The current default activation boundary—after affected work concludes—and explicit interrupt-and-apply-now operation already express this principle. A newly loaded binding should not let an old held gesture silently invoke a new target. The exact policy for a held gesture may be finish under its original mapping, retire it, or explicitly transfer it; its design must specify which.

A late result still belongs to the request, context and environment that produced it. It may be useful to current work without being silently attached as though generated from a newer context. Reconnect to a shared service likewise does not establish that its state was unchanged during absence. Blackbird observes and consumes the service; it does not acquire governance because a UI offers a convenient pause button.

## Predictions, tensions and discriminating cases

These are falsifiable implications and design comparisons, not an ordered implementation plan. Each case attacks the actual claim once, using a directly relevant observation.

| Proposed implication | Case that separates plausible alternatives | Observation that could support or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stable targets reduce wrong-object action during concurrent changes. | Act on a selected running job while new jobs insert and old jobs finish; compare identity anchoring with position anchoring. | Which job actually received the action, ability to predict it, and recovery effort. Identity checking that makes ordinary inspection unusable also counts against the design. |
| Distinguishing acceptance from settlement reduces duplicate or mistaken interventions. | Delay effect settlement and intermittently hide feedback; include completion racing cancellation. | Repeated action attempts, understanding of final/unknown state, completed useful work and time to resume. Extra notices that cost more attention than they save can falsify a proposed presentation. |
| Resource separation matters more than an isolated fast renderer. | Stall output while native work, UI work and input continue; include a long pure-Lua loop and separately a long native callback. | Actual input, handling, effect and feedback events; final semantic outcome and coherent terminal state after drain. A fast average with unusable tails does not answer this case. |
| Coordination views must reveal relationships rather than merely accumulate status. | Find why one collaborator is blocked and choose an intervention using dense status versus linked dependencies/originals. | Correct explanation and target, useful intervention, time/effort, and whether context reconstruction exceeds the saved screen navigation. |
| Programmability needs continuity of meaning. | Change a command mapping while a pedal is held and an older request is pending; then disconnect/reconnect. | No old-generation callback enters retired state; the actual target/effect follows the declared policy; the participant can explain what happened. |
| Human/model surface differences can improve work while preserving shared semantics. | Human selects an exact output and asks a model to reason or act on it through a source reference. | The model receives the intended original/version and can recover missing material without guessing from a screenshot or silently rereading changed files. |

Tradeoffs remain real. Very strict freshness checks can obstruct useful work; aggressive automatic focus can undermine skilled control; more status can obscure consequential change; expressive chords can add ambiguity and delay; default summaries can hide the fact a participant needs; full details can make that fact impossible to find. Rich visuals can improve recognition and enjoyment while consuming work or attention. The theory supplies reasons to evaluate these choices in actual activities, rather than a theorem that one policy wins universally.

## Limits and open conceptual work

Here, observation means access to task-relevant distinctions with stated provenance and uncertainty. It is not the control-theoretic property that an internal state is reconstructible from outputs. Likewise, ability to steer useful work is not mathematical reachability of every system state. A control-theory claim would require an explicit state/dynamics/input/output model and relevant assumptions. None is supplied by merely calling the interface a loop.

The unit of joint work cannot be inferred perfectly from a transcript. Nor does this report establish numerical response envelopes, measure model understanding, prove that every activity benefits from full symmetry, or select a spatial layout. Those questions belong to the integrated human/model/instrument theory and subsequent reviewed design. The strongest justified requirement is concrete: preserve identifiable work, causal action meaning, usable intervention and repair across the concurrency and change that Blackbird explicitly intends to support.
