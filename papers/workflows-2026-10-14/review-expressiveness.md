# Independent design attack: expressiveness, lifecycle and competitive bar

2026-10-14. Layer-one attack of [CONCURRENT_DURABLE_WORKFLOWS.md](../../docs/CONCURRENT_DURABLE_WORKFLOWS.md), read in full, against the [fresh agent workflow source study](agent-workflows.md). Read-only review; no implementation, tests, providers, dependencies or accounts invoked. The root integrates repairs, then one focused recheck follows. This report does not edit the design or create a new assurance gate.

**Assessment:** the proposed destination meets the major competitive feature families and does not falsely claim novelty for Pi Durable's existing mechanisms. Its explicit effects, independent scheduling, context originals, reuse/demand graph and resource ownership have the right ambition. The serious remaining issues concern how those mechanisms compose during live change, rather than missing another workflow diagram or product catalog. Five specification repairs are required; one API illustration would materially strengthen reviewability.

## F1 — High: demand invalidation does not revoke live derived publication

The artifacts section specifies actual read revisions, reverse invalidation and a new run revision/fork when prior outputs change. Publication checks a target base and workspace lease. Epoch checks reject stale incarnations after migration. It does not require a still-live node to prove that the **input revisions and demand revision that justified its result remain current** before publishing into the current graph. Participant generation is insufficient: a worker can remain the same generation while its upstream input changes.

**Adversarial scenario:** reviewer R reads source artifact A1. A1 changes to A2; the demand graph invalidates the report and starts R2. R finishes its A1 report, passes the same output schema and still holds its workspace lease. The target source base itself has not changed. R publishes into the current report alias, and a pending quorum counts it alongside A2 reviewers. The graph now presents a mixed-revision decision as current. With an external publication the same race sends an obsolete announcement despite correct task identity.

**Minimal repair:** define a publication eligibility tuple containing run/demand revision, node/result revision, exact consumed input/context revisions and applicable publication epoch. Invalidation removes current-demand eligibility from active descendants before admitting replacements. Old results remain retained and can be explicitly selected/imported under their old lineage, but cannot update current aliases or satisfy current predicates automatically. A result-ready event and qualifying decision name that tuple. Outputs become visible as current only through a version-checked internal publication. External dispatch rechecks eligibility at its release boundary; invalidation after dispatch creates an actual outstanding effect, not fictional cancellation. The invalidating decision records whether ongoing computation drains, cancels or remains reusable as old work.

This repair should also specify retraction: retracting an upstream stream result invalidates its dependent **derived publications**, but does not erase a previously performed effect or rewrite an already committed join. A new decision/revision can supersede the selection and compensate where policy allows.

## F2 — High: joins over open expansions need separate membership, selection and closure rules

The joins section correctly distinguishes result readiness from loser settlement and says open quorum requires a completion/impossibility rule. However, "a member set or a precisely named closing expansion" and "Expansion can add members only before its retained closing record" leave important interactions unresolved. Closing a producer's expansion is not necessarily closing one consumer's join. Multiple joins may observe the same expansion. A quorum may succeed before expansion closure, while `all` cannot determine its final members. A qualifying predicate may also depend on a revisioned artifact that later retracts.

**Adversarial scenario:** discovery E produces three test shards over time. An early quorum selects two successes and cancels its losers while another `collect` consumes all discovery results. Does quorum close E, merely freeze its own selected set, or allow new shards to become losers? If E remains open, can its new children be cancelled by a previously selected join? If quorum closes E, it unexpectedly breaks the collector. If E's own closing segment waits for `all(E)`, the join and producer are cyclic. A completed failure that a supervisor later retries can also make a naïve quorum claim permanent impossibility too early.

**Minimal repair:** specify an expansion identity with a retained membership revision and independent close/abort records. A join names its observed expansion and a membership acceptance policy; it cannot close a shared producer implicitly. `all`/final collection wait for declared closure and final member outcomes. First/quorum joins can select before closure only under an explicit policy, freezing their own selection and recording what happens to later members. Loser ownership/actions apply to an identified eligible set; future membership requires a separate retained action. Impossibility follows final outcomes plus closed membership or a declared finite bound. Retrying a final node result creates a successor result revision; it cannot mutate an already selected outcome. Predicates are pure pinned functions over retained inputs or separately recorded decisions, never unrecorded model judgments. Detect closure-dependency cycles through the existing wait graph.

## F3 — Medium: durable application state has no clear authored atomic mutation surface

Pi Durable commits application documents, task state and transcript changes together, with revisioned fork policies. Blackbird has strong native retention and atomic decision batches, but the authoring surface explicitly gives a segment **its own immutable state** and proposes outputs/waits/messages. It never states whether authored workflows can maintain typed shared objects—plans, task boards, partition cursors, mutable application settings—or atomically update several such objects with the segment transition. An artifact publication can approximate this, but the specification should state the exact semantics rather than relying on inference from a general-purpose language.

**Adversarial scenario:** two participants complete independent tasks. Each must add its result to a shared plan, advance a task cursor, append a message and release its lease atomically. Pure planning reads plan revision 10 twice. Without explicit compare-and-update publication, one completion overwrites the other's plan. If message/task transition persists separately from plan state, recovery observes incompatible versions. Forking the campaign also needs to say whether this plan is inherited as of the fork, current or fresh.

**Minimal repair:** expose versioned typed application cells/objects through the existing retained owner, not another database. A planning read returns retained value plus revision; the plan can declare an atomic bounded set of expected-revision updates alongside task/wait/inbox changes. Conflicting revisions reject the pure plan before any external dispatch, with retry/replan semantics. Define object ownership, serialization, retention and fork policy. If per-actor state is the selected implementation, explicitly state that a shared object is a named actor and that multi-object atomic updates are supported within one owner—or explicitly exclude them and explain the stronger replacement behavior. Outside services remain separately governed and cannot join this transaction merely because a handle names them.

## F4 — Medium: ordinary durable recovery versus recreation identity is ambiguous

The design says durable orchestration recovers without inventing another effect identity and describes cold revival with generation checks. It then says "Recreation has a successor identity" and rejects replies addressed to an old run. The distinction between normal process recovery, cold-revival of the same durable participant, explicit replacement and killed identity needs to be stated. Otherwise one implementer can preserve all identities while another assigns successor actors and breaks accepted messages/waits after restart.

**Adversarial scenario:** a standing worker accepts message M and starts request P. Controller crashes. Parent has a durable wait and a reporter addressed to that logical participant. Cold revival assigns a successor identity; M and P's response remain on the old inbox and never reach the reporter. Alternatively, discovery restores a killed participant as the same identity and its tombstone no longer blocks revival. Both interpretations can claim to implement "recreation".

**Minimal repair:** normal durable recovery preserves logical participant/run/node/invocation identity and accepted durable inbox endpoints while creating a new **execution incarnation/owner epoch**. Controls against live workers and callbacks include the exact incarnation. New input can target a stable logical endpoint when policy permits; it is retained for the same actor across downtime, with explicit observation/admission semantics. A killed logical actor has terminal retirement/tombstone behavior and cannot auto-revive. Explicit replacement creates a linked successor actor, with individually declared transfer of accepted inbox/waits and unresolved effects. Display-name aliases never silently transfer replies. Clarify whether a kill retires the actor or only stops one incarnation; these must be distinct controls.

This matches Oh My Pi's concrete generation/tombstone discipline and Pi Durable's durable endpoint continuity without merging independent mutable conversations.

## F5 — Medium: implicit resource release at waits needs an executable contract

The resource section correctly releases runnable CPU/provider slots for parked coordinators and forbids a parent holding an exclusive buildtree needed by its child. The illustrative API does not say which reservations belong to an invocation, live effect, actor or workflow, or which `await`/park operations release them. Resource acquisition sets avoid partial acquisition deadlock, but do not by themselves identify that a parent is waiting for work needing its own retained permit.

**Adversarial scenario:** a tool-using agent gets the only provider lane and a writable workspace permit, calls a nested model reviewer, then waits. If its provider lane belongs to the full parent task, the reviewer cannot run. If every wait releases every reservation, the parent loses exclusive workspace protection while a shell process it launched still writes. A workflow holding one executor permit similarly waits for a remote command that needs that permit. These are normal model-authored programs, not rare misuse.

**Minimal repair:** distinguish scheduler-running slots, per-request execution permits, ongoing-effect custody/cleanup credit and explicit durable resource leases. Suspension returns only runnable/request permits whose executions actually ended; it never releases a lease still used by an effect. Nested calls acquire their own request permit from the shared root grants after parent inference returns. Each wait declares/derives its held resource set and owned-effect users. The wait graph diagnoses known lease/self-dependency conflicts before installation and returns the exact release/transfer/split alternative. Include this behavior in the recorded-command convenience API, so authors do not have to invent a custom permit protocol for ordinary nested agent work.

## F6 — Improvement: show a complete recorded-command authoring example

The specification gives a start/read example and one segment plan, but no complete recorded-command program—the surface most likely to determine model ergonomics. Exact spelling can be settled later; the semantics should be reviewable now.

**Suggested illustration:** one Lua function opens a named scope, starts keyed tool-using participants and commands, dynamically expands a keyed task set, awaits a schema-validated quorum, explicitly drains/transfers losers, publishes an artifact with consumed revisions, and exits only after selected settlement. Show timeout/cancellation results and a nested durable callable. State whether await resumes by throwing, returning a typed outcome or invoking a handler; specify whether cancelling the wait cancels the work. Show where semantic keys come from and how repeated loop iterations remain distinct. The same program should be discoverable/callable from model, CLI and station, with retained program identity.

Without this example the destination can remain technically expressive yet force models into manually authored transition bookkeeping for every routine operation. Pi Durable already offers composable tool calls and sequential concurrent code mode. Blackbird needs convenience wrappers that preserve the stronger contracts, not merely a list of primitives.

## Areas that withstand this attack

- Competitive framing is disciplined: no unsupported global superiority or baseline novelty claims. Pi Durable and fresh OMP/CrewAI behaviors are acknowledged rather than compared against obsolete dossiers.
- Tool-using heterogeneous participants, arbitrary configured provider-to-provider routing, independent contexts and peer communication are required; they are not accidentally deferred to a generic connector catalog.
- Full refit custody, SSH/E2EE transport and cross-host storage protocols are correctly separated from local durable task recovery. The design retains required interfaces and explicitly limits guarantees; pretending these protocols already work would be weaker.
- Foreign formats require a semantic support manifest and rejection of unsupported assumptions. A client handle is not equated with local implementation or control of the remote service.
- Staged manifests and explicit interrupt-and-apply honor the operator's affected-workflow conclusion boundary while retaining an escape for infinite station work. A checkpoint is correctly denied automatic authority to replace a workflow.
- Named standing scopes, attach/detach clients, protected cleanup capacity and actual cancellation dispositions support long-lived operation without turning UI presence into execution ownership.
- Model decisions are data from effects; schema validity and quality judgments are not confused with independent correctness. No universal review/approval team is imposed.
- Performance has required mechanisms and measured useful workloads, not hypothetical language-speed superiority or an arbitrary memory ceiling.

## Focused recheck scope

After root integrates accepted changes, recheck only: live invalidation/publication tuple; open membership/selection/closure; authored atomic state updates; recovery/incarnation/replacement identity; reservation lifetime under nested awaits; and completeness of the authoring illustration. No product test campaign or broad repeated competitor research is warranted for these text repairs.
